# How to Spot a Roofing Scam After a Houston Storm

A big storm passes through Houston, and within a day the door-knockers arrive. Some are honest local roofers trying to help. Some are not roofers at all — they are salesmen who will take your money, do poor work, and be gone before the next rain. After a storm, telling the two apart matters more than the price.

## The warning signs

The first sign is pressure. A roofer who says the offer expires today, who wants a signature on the doorstep, or who warns that your roof will collapse if you wait a week is selling fear, not roofing. A real inspection can wait a day for you to think. The second sign is money up front: a demand for full payment before any work starts, or a request to sign over your insurance cheque, is how storm scams work. The third is the "free roof" promise — nobody gives away a roof, and the catch is usually an inflated claim or work that never happens.

## The checks that take ten minutes

Ask for proof of insurance and a local business address, and check both. A roofer with no local address and no insurance is a roofer you cannot find when the work fails. Ask for two recent local references and actually call them. Get the estimate in writing — scope, materials, price, and warranty — before anyone climbs on your roof. And get a second opinion: if one roofer says you need a full replacement and another says a repair will do, the truth is usually in the middle, and a second look costs nothing.

## Do not sign anything on the first visit

No honest roofer needs your signature on the first knock. Read any document before you sign it, especially anything that assigns your insurance claim to the contractor — signing that away means you lose control of your own claim. Take photos of the damage yourself, keep copies of every document, and tell your insurer about the damage before a contractor files anything on your behalf.

## After the storm, slow down

The storm is over in hours, but a bad roofing decision lasts for years. The roofers who do good work in Houston will still be there next week, and the week after. Take the time to check, compare and choose properly — a roof is too expensive to buy from a stranger in a hurry.
