# Metal Roofing in Houston: What It Costs Per Square Foot and Who It Suits

Metal is the roof Houston homeowners ask about after their second shingle replacement — usually standing in a yard looking at granules in the gutter, doing the maths on doing this again in fifteen years. The honest answer starts with the per-square-foot numbers, because metal lives in a different price band from shingle and pretending otherwise wastes everyone's time.

## What metal costs, next to the alternatives

Installed prices per square foot in the Houston area:

- **3-tab shingles:** $3–$5 per sq ft — the budget option, and the shortest-lived.
- **Architectural shingles:** $4–$6 per sq ft — what most Houston homes carry now.
- **Metal roofing:** $5–$16 per sq ft — the wide range is real: exposed-fastener panels sit at the low end, standing seam at the high end.
- **Tile:** $7–$25 per sq ft — heavier, and the structure has to agree to carry it.

For context, a full shingle replacement on a typical Houston home runs $6,712–$13,123, averaging about $9,752. A standing-seam metal roof on the same house can land at double that. So why does anyone buy it?

## Who metal actually suits

**The "last roof" buyer.** Metal roofs routinely outlast two shingle roofs. If you're staying in the house for decades, the second shingle replacement you never buy closes much of the price gap.

**The storm-weary.** Properly fastened metal handles Houston wind and hail country well, and it sheds the kind of wind-driven rain that finds every weak shingle edge during a Gulf storm.

**The heat watcher.** Light-coloured metal reflects a meaningful share of the sun a dark shingle roof absorbs. In a city where the AC runs eight months a year, that shows up in the attic temperature and the electricity bill.

## Who it doesn't suit

If you're selling within five years, you'll pay the premium and the buyer will enjoy it. If the roof shape is chopped into many small sections, valleys and penetrations, labour eats the material advantage. And if the budget is tight this year, a sound architectural shingle roof beats a cheap metal job done badly — installation quality matters more with metal, not less.
